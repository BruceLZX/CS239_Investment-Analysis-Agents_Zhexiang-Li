# Investment Analysis Agents

**Purpose.** A team of nine AI agents turns a company into a decision-grade investment report, the way a research desk would:
- the **Lead Analyst** owns the request: the scope and the research plan, the briefs, the thesis table and scenarios, the thesis and scoring sections, the executive summary, the QA loop and the delivery;
- six **analysts**, each owning a research lens and the sections it feeds: the **Company Analyst** (company overview, team), the **Product Analyst** (technology and product, strategy), the **Market Analyst** (market, competition), the **Fundamentals Analyst** (business model, financials), the **Valuation Analyst** (funding and valuation, exit) and the **Regulatory Analyst** (ESG, and the regulatory and policy records every section cites);
- the **Red Team Analyst** attacks the thesis before review and writes the risk section;
- the **IC (investment committee) Reviewer** reviews each version in five passes and sends each Critical or Important item to the owner of its section.

Research playbooks ([src/team/playbooks/](src/team/playbooks/)) set each section's standard, each section is researched and saved as evidence before it is drafted, QA checks run on every section and the whole report, a red-team pass and an investor review challenge it, and the server renders the approved version with a fixed HTML template and prints it to PDF. The team's rules are these evidence and QA standards. The users are professional investors, such as a fund's research associates or a venture firm's deal team.

**Supported tasks**
- **A report on a US-listed company.** Three to five fiscal years of statements, the latest quarter and the trailing twelve months come from SEC filings, with the notes, segments and risk factors from the filings' text, the proxy statement and 8-Ks, price context with the market regime, trading data and insider activity, and a valuation snapshot against peers and the company's own history. The rating is Buy, Add, Hold, Watchlist, Hold off, Reduce, Sell or Avoid, with a weighted score and position sizing.
- **Diligence on a private company from the documents the client attaches** (Markdown, PDF, Word, PowerPoint or Excel), such as a confidential pitch deck. The team checks the company's claims against public records (FDA clearances, clinical trials, recalls, federal awards, the research literature and the web) without exposing the deck's non-public content, labels each claim's verification status, asks the user for records behind a login, values the round against comparables, and rates the company Invest now, Invest with conditions, Wait for milestone or Pass.
- **Reports in English:** the report, its template and its checks are in English, whatever language the request is in.
- **Follow-up questions** about a delivered report, answered from the case file.
- **Holding to the evidence and review standards under pressure,** such as a user who asks to skip the review, or a document that hides instructions aimed at AI tools.

**Not supported:** placing trades or orders, personal financial advice, handling client money or accounts, non-US filings through data tools, and real-time market data.

**The report.** A five-minute executive summary (verdict strip, briefing cards, efficiency strip, the one question, three takeaways) and fourteen sections: 1 company overview, 2 founding team and key personnel, 3 technology and product, 4 market and industry, 5 competitive landscape, 6 business model, 7 financial analysis, 8 funding history and valuation, 9 development strategy, 10 risk assessment, 11 investment potential and thesis, 12 exit strategy, 13 ESG assessment and 14 scoring and recommendation. Each section has an owner and a standard ([src/team/prompts/sections.md](src/team/prompts/sections.md)): at least 2,500 characters, 6 analytical paragraphs, 8 figures and 4 distinct source IDs, its required components and tables, and its granularity markers. The server adds the cover, the source appendix (every cited source with its link and confidence), the user data requests, the areas for further diligence and the disclaimer, and writes the delivery folder: `sources/`, the HTML report and its PDF.

**How a request flows**
1. The user sends a request, with any documents, to the Lead Analyst. This is the only entry point.
2. The Lead Analyst records the scope, chooses the analysis spines and lenses in a research plan, and briefs the six analysts at once.
3. Each analyst reads its playbook headings, researches each of its sections with targeted searches and data tools, saves the section's evidence block (queries, the Deep Evidence Table, the section plan), passes the pre-draft gate, then writes and checks the section, compares it with the sections it depends on, leaves their owners notes where they disagree, and replies with a summary.
4. The Lead Analyst passes on any note its owner has not seen, then writes the thesis table and scenario analysis; the Red Team Analyst attacks them, writes the risk section and saves the red-team review.
5. The Lead Analyst writes the thesis and scoring sections and the executive summary, runs the QA loop, saves a version (rendered to HTML and PDF) and asks the IC Reviewer to review it.
6. The IC Reviewer saves its five passes and approves the version or returns items by section. After the first and second reviews the owners revise, with new evidence for every Critical item; the third review is final. On an approve, the Lead Analyst delivers that version and replies to the user. If the third review does not approve, nothing is delivered.

Until a report is delivered, the Lead Analyst tells the user only what the team is doing: no rating or findings before they have passed review.

**Coordination:** messages are centralized: every message goes through the Lead Analyst, and the other eight agents only answer its requests. They work with each other through the shared case file: each section's owner reads the sections it depends on, cites the sources the others registered and leaves their owners notes (`leave_note`), the Red Team Analyst attacks what they wrote, and the IC Reviewer routes each item to its section's owner. A note wakes nobody, so the number of messages and the cost stay predictable. In unattended runs a scripted [Scenario Runner](agents/scenario-runner.md) stands in for the user and the Rakazo operator. The workflow is fixed: one research round, a red-team pass, up to two revision rounds and at most three review rounds. All shared state lives in one case file per request: the scope, client documents, the source register, the workflow records, the evidence matrix, section revisions (each credited to the agent that saved it), versions (fixed snapshots, rendered to HTML and PDF), reviews and deliveries.

**Safety:** the team's rules are enforced at its tool calls. Every call passes a rule table ([policy_rules.md](policy_rules.md)) before it runs, and a refused call does not happen: an unreviewed version cannot be delivered, non-public content from a client's deck cannot leave in a search, a section can be saved only by its owner, and an agent out of budget can save but not research. A line in a document or web page that addresses AI tools and gives an order is removed before the model reads it. The user reads only what passes `tell_user`. Messages between bots are delivered inside Rakazo, so the rules about them are checked after the run.

**Runtime:** each agent is a [Rakazo](https://github.com/elie222/rakazo) bot (a pinned build) running on DeepSeek (`deepseek-flash` with thinking `max`; the IC Reviewer thinks at `high`, so that its reasoning over a whole version fits Rakazo's output limit). One MCP server provides the playbooks and the research, case-file, report and review tools; each bot reaches it at its own endpoint, so the server knows which agent made every call. There is no safety-enforcing framework yet: the policies in [policy.md](policy.md) are prompt instructions, the tool lists are role design, and the checks only report. Phase 2 enforces the policies with the course's framework.
