## 2. The problem
Strawberry growers in California and Florida lose an estimated 10-15% of marketable fruit each season because they cannot hire enough pickers at peak harvest. Harvest labor is the largest single cost line for most growers.

- Strawberries are picked by hand every three to four days through a season of up to seven months; a picker covers roughly a quarter of an acre a day (company estimate).
- Growers tell us their crews are smaller and older each year, while wages and worker housing keep getting more expensive (grower interviews, 2025).
- Fruit left unpicked rots on the plant and spreads mold to the next pass, so a short crew costs more than the berries it leaves behind.
- Harvest aids such as carts and conveyors make pickers faster, but still need a full crew.

## 3. Our product: the Orrin Picker
- An autonomous, track-mounted robot that drives the furrows at night and day, finds ripe berries with stereo cameras and a ripeness model, and picks them with a soft three-finger gripper.
- Pick rate: **94% of ripe fruit** on the rows tested in our 2025 pilots (company measurement; definition on slide 4).
- Throughput: one robot covers roughly 1.5 acres per 24 hours.
- Two picking arms place berries straight into the grower's retail clamshells on board, so fruit is handled once.
- Runs about 20 hours a day on swappable battery packs; one field technician supervises up to 8 robots, on site or remotely.
- Sold as Robots-as-a-Service: growers pay per robot per season; we own, maintain and insure the fleet.
- Current model: Orrin Picker v3, released to the field in March 2026. The v4, designed for lower cost, is planned for the 2027 season.

## 4. Technology and field performance
| Metric (2025 pilots, company measurement) | Result |
|---|---|
| Pick rate: ripe berries picked / ripe berries detected | 94% |
| Rows tested | 212 rows at 9 of the 14 pilot farms |
| Berries bruised or punctured in picking | 3.8% |
| Unripe berries picked by mistake | 2.1% |
| Picking speed | 5.6 seconds a berry, per arm |
| Uptime in scheduled hours | 86% |
| Average time between operator interventions | 3.4 hours |

- Perception: two stereo camera pairs per arm, with LED lighting for night work. The ripeness model was trained on 1.9 million labelled berry images from the Calder University lab and our pilots.
- Gripper: three silicone fingers with slip sensing; the stem is cut, not pulled.
- Navigation: furrow following without GPS, so robots keep working in coastal fog and at night.

Definitions: "ripe berries detected" are the berries the vision system classified as ripe. Test rows were chosen by Orrin's field team; rows under plastic tunnels, with standing water or on slopes over 4% were excluded.

## 7. Market size (company estimates)
- **$42B** global fresh-produce harvesting labor market.
- **$3.1B** serviceable market: US strawberry and other soft-fruit harvesting.
- **$310M** obtainable by 2031: 10% of the serviceable market.
- US strawberry acreage is concentrated: the top 20 growers farm over half of California's acreage.
- Bottom-up: about 50,000 US strawberry acres need about 14,300 robots at one robot per 3.5 acres. At the 2027 list price of $16,500 a robot-season that is about $236M a year from strawberries alone, before raspberries, blackberries and blueberries.
- Adoption assumed in our plan: 2% of US strawberry acreage by 2028 and 15% by 2031.

## 8. Competition (as we see it)
- We are **the only autonomous strawberry picker with a pick rate above 90%** in open-field conditions.

| Approach | Open-field strawberries | Who picks | Pick rate | Business model |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Orrin Picker | Yes, day and night | The robot | 94% (2025 pilots) | Robots-as-a-Service, per robot-season |
| Greenhouse and tabletop robots | No: raised tabletop beds only | The robot | Not comparable | Equipment sale or service |
| Picker-assist platforms | Yes | People, riding a moving platform | Same as a hand crew | Equipment sale or lease |
| Early-stage research robots | Field trials only | The robot | Below 80% in published trials (our review) | Not yet sold |

- Why we win: night picking doubles the hours each robot works; the soft gripper keeps bruising close to hand-picking; Robots-as-a-Service means no capital outlay for the grower; our COO's grower relationships open doors.
- What we watch: well-funded greenhouse robot companies moving into open fields, and equipment makers adding picking arms to harvest-aid platforms.

## 9. Business model and unit economics
- Growers rent robots by the season: about 7 months, April to October in California and December to March in Florida. Orrin Fleet Services LLC owns, maintains and insures the fleet.
- Price: **$9,500 per robot per season** on 2025 and 2026 contracts. The 2027 list price for new contracts is $16,500; 2027 renewals average $12,700.
- Robot unit cost: **$48,000** (current v3 build; target **$31,000** at 500 units with the v4 design).

| Per robot-season, 2026 to date (company) | Amount | Share of price |
|---|---|---|
| Price | $9,500 | 100% |
| Field service and remote supervision | ($3,100) | 33% |
| Transport, insurance and consumables | ($2,500) | 26% |
| Gross profit | $3,900 | 41% |

- Gross margin: **41%** in 2026 year to date, rising to 60%+ at scale (company projection). Fleet depreciation, straight-line over 4 seasons, is reported in operating expenses, not in gross margin.
- Payback per robot: 2.1 seasons (at the $31,000 target unit cost, the 2027 list price and a target service cost of $1,750 a robot-season).
