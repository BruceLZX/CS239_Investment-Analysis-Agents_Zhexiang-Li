# Orrin Fieldworks: Series A investor presentation

**CONFIDENTIAL: shared under NDA with Meridian Capital Ventures. Do not distribute, forward or disclose any figures in this document.**

## 1. The problem
Strawberry growers in California and Florida lose an estimated 10-15% of marketable fruit each season because they cannot hire enough pickers at peak harvest. Harvest labor is the largest single cost line for most growers.

## 2. Our product: the Orrin Picker
- An autonomous, track-mounted robot that drives the furrows at night and day, finds ripe berries with stereo cameras and a ripeness model, and picks them with a soft three-finger gripper.
- Pick rate: **94% of ripe fruit** on the rows tested in our 2025 pilots (company measurement).
- Throughput: one robot covers roughly 1.5 acres per 24 hours.
- Sold as Robots-as-a-Service: growers pay per robot per season; we own, maintain and insure the fleet.

## 3. Traction
- **14 pilot farms** across California (Salinas, Watsonville, Santa Maria) and Florida (Plant City).
- **2 paid seasonal deployments** converted from pilots in 2025.
- Pilot customers include **Marisol Ridge Farms** and the **Pajaro Bluff Growers Cooperative** (names confidential).
- **Annual recurring revenue: $3.2M** as of September 2026.
- FY2025 revenue: **$1.9M**, up from $0.4M in FY2024.

## 4. Unit economics
- Robot unit cost: **$48,000** (current build; target $31,000 at 500 units).
- Price: **$9,500 per robot per season** (about 7 months).
- Gross margin: **41%** in 2026 year to date, rising to 60%+ at scale (company projection).
- Payback per robot: 2.1 seasons.

## 5. Market
- **$42B** global fresh-produce harvesting labor market.
- **$3.1B** serviceable market: US strawberry and other soft-fruit harvesting.
- US strawberry acreage is concentrated: the top 20 growers farm over half of California's acreage.

## 6. Competition
- We are **the only autonomous strawberry picker with a pick rate above 90%** in open-field conditions.
- Other approaches: greenhouse-only robots, picker-assist platforms that still need human pickers, and early-stage research robots.

## 7. Team

- **Teodor Vashti, COO:** 15 years running harvest operations for large berry growers.

## 8. Financials and the raise
- Monthly net burn: **$410,000**; runway: **9 months** at current burn.
- Raising **$18M Series A at a $72M pre-money valuation**.
- Use of funds: build 300 robots for the 2027 season (55%), expand perception and gripper R&D (25%), sales and field operations (20%).

## 9. Key risks (as we see them)
- Weather and field variability affect pick rates.
- Hardware reliability at fleet scale.
- Grower adoption cycles are tied to the season calendar.
