## 2. The problem
Urgent-care clinics and rural hospitals without an on-site laboratory send cardiac and metabolic blood tests to reference labs and wait 1-3 days for results. Patients with chest pain or uncontrolled diabetes are often transferred, or sent home, before results arrive.

- A transfer for a suspected heart attack that turns out negative costs thousands of dollars and takes a rural ambulance out of service for hours (company estimate).
- Benchtop analyzers that could run these tests on site need venous blood, a trained operator and daily quality control, which small clinics can't staff.
- The clinics we surveyed send out a median of 14 cardiac or metabolic panels a day (company survey of 52 clinics, 2024).

## 3. Our product: the Vantelle Q5
- A handheld, cartridge-based analyzer that runs a five-test cardiac and metabolic panel from a finger-stick sample in **12 minutes**.
- The panel: high-sensitivity troponin I, NT-proBNP, HbA1c, creatinine (with eGFR) and potassium.
- One 40-microliter drop of blood. Each cartridge holds all its reagents and an on-board calibrator, so there is no separate calibration step.
- The analyzer weighs 640 grams, runs on a battery and sends results to the clinic's electronic health record over Wi-Fi (HL7 and FHIR).
- Razor-and-blade model: instruments placed at or near cost; recurring revenue from single-use cartridges.
- Cartridges keep for 9 months at room temperature (our stability data so far covers 6 months).

## 4. Clinical performance
- Lab-grade accuracy: **98.7% agreement** with central-lab results in our internal study of 640 samples (company data).

| Assay | Results compared | Agreement with the central lab | Imprecision (CV) at the decision level |
|---|---|---|---|
| High-sensitivity troponin I | 128 | 96.1% | 11.8% at the 99th-percentile cutoff |
| NT-proBNP | 127 | 98.4% | 7.2% |
| HbA1c | 128 | 99.2% | 2.9% |
| Creatinine | 126 | 100% | 4.1% |
| Potassium | 125 | 100% | 2.4% |
| All five assays | 634 of 640 | 98.7% | |

- Study design: 128 patients at one urgent-care site, September to December 2025. Venous blood samples were run on our analyzer by Vantelle staff and compared with the site's reference laboratory. Six results were left out for insufficient sample or a cartridge error.
- "Agreement" means the result fell in the same clinical category (normal or abnormal) as the central-lab result.
- Pivotal study for the remaining assays and the CLIA waiver: 4 sites, finger-stick samples run by clinic staff, about 1,150 patients; enrolling since July 2026, results expected in Q2 2027.

## 8. Market and competition
- **$18B** global point-of-care diagnostics market; **$2.4B** serviceable market in US urgent care and rural hospitals.
- How we size the serviceable market: about 13,200 sites (11,400 urgent-care centers and 1,800 rural hospitals) x 2 analyzers x 8 panels a day x 300 days x $38 a cartridge.
- Incumbent point-of-care analyzers need venous blood and trained staff; ours runs from a finger stick and needs neither.

## 9. Business model and unit economics
- Analyzer: list price $2,900 against a build cost of $3,350, so we place analyzers at or near cost; 60% of evaluation sites received theirs free.
- Cartridge: list price $38; average realized price $33.40 in 2026; cost **$16.20** at today's volumes, with a target of **$7.50** at 2 million cartridges a year (2029).

| Per cartridge | 2026 | 2029 target |
|---|---|---|
| Average price | $33.40 | $31.00 |
| Cost | $16.20 | $7.50 |
| Gross margin | 51% | 76% |

- After clearance, clinics bill payers for each panel under existing test codes; we sell cartridges to the clinic and do not bill payers ourselves.
- Our model assumes 8 panels a day per analyzer from 2028; evaluation sites used an average of 41 cartridges per site a month in 2026.
